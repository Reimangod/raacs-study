# Study overview

## The question

Can a small, adaptively built bank of sampling circuits supply useful electronic
configurations at a manageable circuit and construction cost? The recorded study
compares the registered RAACS policy with LUCJ and SqDRIFT controls under explicit
sampling and selected-CI settings. It does not establish that any method is best
for every molecule or every computational budget.

## The quantum and classical roles

Quantum circuits supply samples of electronic configurations. In this archive,
those samples were generated by noiseless simulation. Classical CI chooses the
coefficients on the selected configurations and produces the reported energy.
RAACS uses a classical residual/resource heuristic to choose new exchange
operations, samples their circuits, and updates a bank of at most four circuits.
The final classical CI state is not claimed to be prepared by that bank as a
single quantum circuit. See [the method](METHOD.md).

## The minimum fair comparison

Keep the molecule, physical inputs, cohort, final shots, actual determinant
dimension, and spin/readout convention fixed. Then inspect energy together with
CNOT count, depth, initialization, construction/pilot sampling, and classical CI
cost. Equal final shots and D alone do not make the total computational work equal.

Recovery means correlation-energy recovery, not noise repair. Unknown parameter
counts remain unknown. Missing or D-unreached cases remain visible. Readout seeds
do not always correspond to independent source constructions, and repeated
endpoints are not independent experiments.

## What can be reproduced cheaply

The portable CLI verifies the included file hashes and rebuilds saved-data tables
and figures without a GPU or raw-archive download. An optional numerical check
recomputes two saved LiH selected bases. Neither operation repeats the full
acquisition, reruns every historical dispatcher, or certifies all large-system
reference energies. See [verification scope](VERIFICATION.md).

## Storage and publication

The private release contains the larger raw evidence archives. The Git repository
contains the reading guides, catalog, policy snapshots, and lightweight
reproduction tools. Content-addressed storage removes duplicate bytes, not real
execution costs. No Zenodo DOI is assigned; public redistribution requires the
separate [rights review](RIGHTS_AND_PUBLICATION.md).
